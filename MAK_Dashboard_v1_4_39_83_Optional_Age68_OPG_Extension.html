Health insurance / survivor benefits: under the lump-sum path, treat OPG retiree/member benefit continuation as materially weaker than the DB pension path. Based on your note, spouse health insurance benefits end when the primary member passes away. This must be verified directly with OPG before any election.

  • The non-taxable portion likely represents a tax-sheltered transfer/locked-in component, not automatically free spending cash.

FactorWhat to checkDecision read
1. Rate environmentCompare the current CV ÷ annual pension multiple against prior annual tracker values. Rates down usually improve the CV; rates up usually weaken it.A materially richer multiple supports considering commutation. A weak/falling multiple favours the DB pension.

Annual lump-sum tracker

Purpose: capture the OPG commuted/lump-sum quote every annual update so the dashboard can track whether the lump-sum option is improving or deteriorating as you move toward retirement. The key year-over-year signal is CV ÷ annual lifetime pension: if the multiple rises, the quote is richer relative to the DB pension; if it falls, the DB pension is pulling ahead. Treat this as a required private OPG data item alongside DB pension, bridge, CPP, and Joint & 100% survivor values.
Red — you must provide from OPG portalTotal lump sum, taxable portion, non-taxable / transferable portion, termination date, and quote/as-of date for every available pre-65 retirement point.
Green — I can retrieve/updatePublic rate snapshot for context: Bank of Canada overnight target, Government of Canada 10-year yield, and long-bond yield if available.
Amber — dashboard calculatesTaxable percentage, transferable percentage, change since prior captured point, peak quote, and whether the tax-sheltered mix is improving.
Gray — decision noteRecord whether the quote was used for planning only, annual update, or near-retirement decision validation.

What to capture each year

  • Quote/as-of date from the OPG portal.
  • Total lump sum, taxable amount, and non-taxable / transferable amount for each available pre-65 exit date.
  • Whether the non-taxable portion is locked-in, transferable to a LIRA/LIF, or otherwise restricted.
  • Any portal wording about health/retiree benefits, spouse benefit continuation, survivor impact, and expiry of the quote.
  • The actuarial basis shown by the portal: interest-rate basis, indexation basis, and mortality basis.

How to interpret movement

  • If rates fall, the commuted value may rise; if rates rise, it may fall.
  • If the total value rises but the taxable share also rises, the after-tax result may not improve much.
  • If the transferable share rises, the lump-sum path may become more tax-efficient, but it still carries investment and longevity risk.
  • Refresh the quote again close to any real retirement or termination decision.
